Android Tablet Integration Checklist for Software Teams

An Android tablet pilot should prove that your production app works on the exact hardware and firmware you plan to deploy. Start by documenting the app, peripherals and operating environment. Then test provisioning, normal operation, and recovery from common failures before approving a bulk order.

From app brief to pilot sign-off

  1. Specify
    App build, Android version, interfaces and operating conditions.
  2. Configure
    Confirm the device, firmware build, app installation and management setup.
  3. Validate
    Record results for real workflows, recovery and an update rehearsal.

1. What should a software team send before choosing a tablet?

Share the app package name and build version, minimum Android version, and any dependence on Google Play services. List the actual peripherals by make and model, the required ports, mounting and charging needs, and where the tablet will run. If the device needs data and charging through the same connector, make simultaneous operation a test requirement.

Describe what a user does during a normal shift or session. A hotel check-in tablet may need to recover after temporary Wi-Fi loss. A learning tablet may need to preserve a student's work after a restart and keep a stylus responsive during sustained use.

2. What should you test on the pilot unit?

Example acceptance tests to adapt to your deployment

App and startupProvision a reset unit, verify the installed app version, and run the production login and core workflow.Hardware revision, Android build, APK version and expected launch behavior.
Kiosk controlsTry navigation and notifications as a normal user, reboot the tablet, and verify the authorized maintenance exit.Allowed apps, admin recovery procedure and behavior after restart.
Peripherals and powerConnect the actual scanner, dock or accessory. Exercise data transfer during charging when required and repeat after reconnecting it.Peripheral model, cable or dock, and which functions passed.

Updates and managementApply an app or system update on a pilot unit. Recheck enrollment, data retention and kiosk behavior.Update channel, version pair, failure recovery procedure and responsible owner.

3. Who owns provisioning and updates after delivery?

Assign an owner to firmware, app updates and device management before rollout. Android's lock task mode can restrict a dedicated device to an allowlisted app or set of apps when configured through a device policy controller. Verify the enrollment and policy on your target devices. Read Android’s lock task mode documentation.

Keep an approved reference unit and record the firmware build, app version, peripherals and open issues. Agree which changes trigger a retest before the next production batch. Include the recovery steps the deployment team will use if a field device fails.
